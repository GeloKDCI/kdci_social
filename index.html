Why this exists

A better edit cannot rescue a bad package

30%

of time top creators spend on ideation and packaging

5%

of time small operators spend on the same thing

40x

daily views from one thumbnail improved by 30-40%

The proof case

An astrophotography channel went from 2-3K views per video to over 1M. Same camera, same editor, same subject. The only thing that changed was the packaging: "I photographed the Milky Way" became "Photographing the Milky Way in 10 minutes, 1 hour, and 24 hours."

S2

The five rules everything hangs off

1

If they don't click, they don't watch.

Title, first frame and opening line are one unit. Design them together.

2

Respect their time.

Every second advances the story or gets cut. There is no third category.

3

Do they want more stuff like this?

One video's job is to earn the next click, not just this one.

4

Package before you produce.

Write the title and describe frame 0 before the shot list.

5

Steal structure, not content.

Formats travel across niches. A car-channel format is fresh in AI content.

Rules 1-3 adopted verbatim from D13. Rule 5 evidenced by H04.

S3 / Anatomy

Every hook is three layers landing at once

Most misses are a missing layer, not a weak idea. Diagnose before you rewrite.

Visual

Frame 0

0.0s

Does the still communicate topic and stakes?

Text

On-screen overlay

0.3s

Readable in one glance. Six words maximum.

Verbal

First spoken sentence

0.5-3.0s

Contains a problem and a turn.

Sound-off is the default. On LinkedIn, assume it always. The visual and text layers carry the hook alone. Worked example: H01.

Hard gate

The mute test

Play the first three seconds with the sound off.

If the topic and the stakes are not clear, the hook is broken and the video does not ship.

The cheapest quality gate we have, and it takes eleven seconds to run.
Adopted from D12. Every format, every platform, no exceptions.

S3 / Construction

Write out of order, on purpose

Sequence

1

The hook line

2

The last line - the payoff

3

Everything in between

Forces the payoff to exist before the script does. If you cannot write the last line, you do not have a video yet.

The but-then construction

"I went to Chick-fil-A but they were closed, then a man walked up to me."

but = a problem inside one second. then = a loop scrolling cannot close. See H02.

Our versions

  • "We automated our whole thumbnail process, but CTR dropped 40%, then we found out why."
  • "This client wanted 200 videos a month, but they had two editors, so..."

S3 / Timing

What each window has to do

Bar widths are proportional to real duration, so you can see how little of the video the hook actually is.

Vertical - Shorts, Reels, TikTok under 60 seconds

0.0 - 1.0s

Visual and text hook

One subject. High brightness, high saturation. Clean background.

1.0 - 3.0s

Verbal hook completes

Problem and turn both delivered. The "then" has landed.

3.0 - 8.0s

Foreshadow the payoff

Show the mechanism that will resolve it. Do not describe it.

8.0s - end

Deliver in visible steps

Land the payoff on the final second. Loop if under 20s - see the pattern watch.

Long-form - the first minute is the whole game

0:00 - 0:15

Deliver on the thumbnail promise

Show, don't state.

0:15 - 1:00

The unreasonable visual

Something only KDCI would do. Reference: H08.

1:00 - 3:00

Progression

Show what is happening, don't narrate what's coming.

3:00 - 6:00

Fastest section

Highest cut density. Re-engage the drifters.

6:00 - end

Do not coast

Abrupt end, energy high to the last frame.

21M

viewers lost in the first minute of a video that got roughly 60M clicks - and the team calls that above average. Front-loaded drop-off is normal. Judge the shape of the curve, not the size of the cliff.

S3 / Language

Write plainer than feels comfortable

Fifth grade or below

The jargon is what we're cutting, not the intelligence. "AI procurement platform" is fine. "Agentic orchestration layer" is not.

Specific numbers beat round ones

2,847 beats "thousands". Every figure must be defensible - B2B audiences check. See H06.

Power words, sparingly

free / banned / secret / wrong / stopped / fired. Twice a month, not twice a week.

Credibility is the constraint

A hook that wins TikTok and costs us a qualified lead is a net loss. When in doubt, the restrained option.

Provisional. Basis: D12.

S4 / Pacing

Editing rules

3s

No shot longer than 3 seconds in the first 15.

5s

After that, nothing over 5 seconds without motion inside the frame.

0s

Zero intro. No logo sting, no "hey guys". The wordmark lands at the end.

  • Cut on the word, not after it - trailing air is our most common dead space.
  • One idea per shot. Two ideas means two shots.
  • Trim the last second aggressively. Watch the tail of the curve, not just the head.
  • Design sub-20s Reels to loop. Match last frame to first - a free second view.
  • Foreshadow, don't preview. Show the object; never announce what's coming.

Cut density: vertical under 30s means 8-14 cuts. Long-form first minute means a scene change every 2-3 seconds.

S6

Frame 0 and the thumbnail are the same problem

One still image, competing for one glance.

01

One subject

Two is already too many.

02

Bright and saturated

Dark and muted loses in a bright feed.

03

Clean background

Isolate or blur. Busy kills the read.

04

Legible at 15%

Shrink it. If you can't tell what it is, redo it.

05

Four words maximum

And it must extend the title, not repeat it.

06

Familiar but unexpected

Legible in the niche, odd enough to stop a scroll.

Process: three first-frame options per video. Shrink all three to 15%. Pick blind. Log the winner.

Process

Logging an entry takes five minutes

Watch only the first ten seconds. Then watch it again on mute.

Screenshot frame 0 - the true first frame - and drop it in the shared folder as /frames/<ID>.png.

Describe frame 0 literally. What is physically on screen, no interpretation.

Tag the hook type from the 14. If nothing fits, propose a new type at the bi-monthly - do not invent one inline.

Write the why in one or two lines, naming the mechanism rather than the vibe. Good: "opens on the finished result, so the whole video is a reveal of how." Bad: "really punchy energy."

Write the KDCI application. If you cannot, drop the row.

Flip the status to sourced and verified, and add your initials and the date.

Target: 26 exemplar rows minimum, capped at 40. Each bi-monthly, add 4, retire 2, and promote at least one pattern into the standard.

Honesty

What this version does not have yet

1

No KDCI baseline data

Our own best and worst performer, and our motion-kit inventory, are still unlogged - so every provisional number here is borrowed from other creators. Biggest hole by far, and both rows are held back from the library until they are filled.

2

8 of 26 rows unverified

Nobody at KDCI has watched them with the framework in hand. Filter the library by "to verify" to see the exact queue.

3

LinkedIn under-covered

Arguably our most important platform, and the library has almost nothing native to it.

4

No thumbnail A/B process

The vidIQ method in D07 is a critique process, not a test. We need a real one.

5

Two pattern-watch figures are second-hand

The format shelf-life and scroll-past numbers came via secondary reporting. Verify before quoting either externally.

Bring the video and the retention graph to the bi-monthly. Rules change there, not in the edit. A pattern becomes a rule after it works on three KDCI videos. Not one.
